# LinkedIn post for ProjectForge

## The post (copy and paste)

Writing a final-year project report is hard for a reason that has nothing to do with engineering.

Nobody shows you what goes where.

You get a PDF of guidelines and a slide deck, and then a blank page. What does the Statement of the Problem actually need to say? Where do the roman page numbers start? Why is my table of contents wrong again?

So I built something to fix that. It's called ProjectForge.

You pick your project type (design and construction, or maintenance and repair), and your whole report is already set up the way the department wants it. Every section has a short guide that dims the page and walks you through what examiners look for, one step at a time. It never writes the report for you.

It also handles the parts that eat your time:
• References in IEEE or APA, and you can switch whenever you like
• Figures and tables numbered for you
• Worked calculations that show the working properly
• A "book view" so you can flip through your report before you print it
• One tap to download a Word file with the right margins, font, spacing and page numbers

I designed it for the phone, because that's where most of us actually write.

It's still early. I'm looking for a few EEE students to try it and tell me honestly where they get stuck.

If you're writing your project right now (or you supervise someone who is), have a look: [your link]

Built with the help of AI coding tools, and a lot of reading of our own guidelines.

## Posting tips

1. LinkedIn lets one post carry a video OR a set of images, not both together.
   - Option A: post the video as the main post, and share the images in a second post a day or two later.
   - Option B: post the PDF carousel (ProjectForge-carousel.pdf, 7 slides) as the main post, and put the video link in the first comment.
2. Put the link in the first comment, not the post. LinkedIn tends to show posts with links in the body to fewer people.
3. Post on a weekday morning, and reply to every comment in the first hour.
4. Tag your department, your supervisor or classmates only if they're happy to be tagged.
5. Replace [your link] before you post.
